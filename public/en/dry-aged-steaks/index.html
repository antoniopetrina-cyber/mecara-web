What is dry ageing?

A whole piece of beef, usually on the bone, hangs for weeks in its own room. The temperature sits just above freezing, and humidity and airflow are kept under control. That's dry ageing.

The meat slowly loses water, which concentrates the flavour. Natural enzymes work on the fibres at the same time and soften them. You end up with a more tender steak and a deeper, slightly nutty taste that fresh beef simply doesn't have.

The outside dries out and gets trimmed off before we cut. Hence the higher price per kilo. Some weight is lost, and it takes weeks.

How long should it age?

around 21 days

Milder, very juicy. A good place to start if aged beef is new to you.

28 – 35 days

Fuller and more pronounced, and noticeably more tender. This is what most people go for at a steak dinner.

45 days and more

Intense and distinctive. For people who already know they love aged steak.

Treat these as a rough guide. The cut, the fat and the piece itself all affect the flavour, so ask the butcher what they'd pick for your meal.

Cooking a dry-aged steak

  • Take it out of the fridge 30 to 60 minutes before cooking so it comes up to room temperature.
  • Salt it right before it goes in, or at least 40 minutes ahead. Pepper goes on at the end.
  • Get the pan or grill very hot. Sear until there's a crust, then finish over lower heat if it needs it.
  • Rest it for 5 to 10 minutes. Slice across the grain.
  • Thick, bone-in cut? Ask the butcher about timing and doneness.

Questions and answers

Do you have dry-aged steaks?

Have a look at the dry-aged steak page, or ask the butcher what's in. If you're after a particular piece, confirm the cut, ageing and weight before you come in.

Can I ask for a specific thickness?

Put the thickness or how you want it cut in your enquiry. We'll tell you if that works for the piece you picked.

Why is the total only an estimate?

Anything sold by weight is charged on what it actually weighs, at the confirmed price per kilo. Got a budget limit? Say so in the enquiry.
